What we're proposing

A system page that starts from the engineering problem (no mechanical link between the handwheel and the rack), shows the architecture as a clickable block diagram, and lets the engineer pick the block they are responsible for. Each block explains what matters for that socket, suggests parts, and hands over to the product table with the right filters already applied. Variants of the architecture sit on tabs rather than separate pages.

Why

This is the journey the VP of Marketing will recognise from Texas Instruments: Application, System, Block diagram, Product category, Device. Allegro is most of the way there already. The gap is that today's diagram pop-ups are a list of part numbers with no reason given and no route into the wider range, and the A1335 (which lists steering as a target application) doesn't appear on the steer-by-wire page at all.

Infineon's steering page does one thing worth taking: a single diagram with tabs for steer-by-wire, rear-wheel steering and fail-operational EPS, and a "download BOM" for each.

Steer-by-Wire

Only the steer-by-wire system is built in this prototype, so its content is shown here.

With no steering column, the handwheel and the road wheels are joined only by sensors, software and motors. Every sensing socket has to be fail-operational, and the driver still expects to feel the road.

Design requirements

  • Redundant, ASIL D angle and torque sensing
  • Low latency between handwheel and rack
  • Stray field immunity next to high-current motors
  • 12V and 48V supply options
